Beyond Rules:
Why Data Quality Needs More Than Deterministic Validation

From regulatory compliance to intelligent risk discovery

In financial services, data quality validation has traditionally relied on deterministic rules. These controls provide transparency, consistency, and an auditable foundation. But as institutions adopt AI, an important question emerges.

If deterministic rules already identify data quality failures, why do we need machine learning?

And if ML discovers a failure, why not simply fix the rule?
01

Rules Validate What We Know

Deterministic rules are effective when regulatory requirements are explicit. In FR 2052a liquidity reporting, they validate classifications, maturity assignments, collateral eligibility, and other prescribed conditions. Yet a record can pass every formal check while misrepresenting the underlying transaction. Compliance with known rules is not always proof of data correctness.

02

Why Not Fix the Rule?

If an investigation reveals a repeatable error with a clear root cause, correct the source issue and strengthen the deterministic control. Machine learning should not become an expensive way to rediscover known violations. But some failures arise from interacting attributes, historical behavior, and changing business patterns. Although complex rules can represent many relationships, maintaining them with acceptable false-positive rates may be difficult.

03

Supervised Learning Generalizes Experience

Imagine a maturity-bucket misclassification in FR 2052a. A rule can prevent that particular mapping error. A supervised model trained on independently confirmed investigations may identify related failures with different transaction characteristics or upstream causes. It estimates which cases resemble known errors, rather than merely checking explicit violations. If its labels are generated only by existing rules, however, the model mostly learns to imitate those rules. Its added value must be demonstrated against a rules-only baseline.

04

Not Every Anomaly Is a Data Quality Failure

A data quality anomaly means reported information incorrectly describes the institution's position. A liquidity risk signal means the information may be correct but reveals a concentration, exposure, or emerging pattern requiring attention. A genuine rise in overnight funding concentration, for example, belongs with liquidity risk management—not a data remediation queue. Intelligent validation must distinguish these outcomes.

05

An Architecture Beyond Rules

Combine deterministic controls for explicit requirements, unsupervised discovery for unusual patterns, supervised learning for confirmed-error patterns, contextual explanations for investigators, and human governance for final decisions. Model scores are signals to investigate, not proof of an error. Confirmed findings should feed back into source corrections, rules, training labels, and risk escalation.

06

The Real Transformation

The objective is not rules versus AI. It is a continuously improving control framework that knows when to enforce a rule, when to learn from investigated experience, when to explore an unexplained pattern, and when to escalate a genuine business risk.

“Rules validate what we know. Supervised learning generalizes investigated experience. Intelligent discovery challenges what we have not yet considered.”

The future is not rules versus AI.

It is rules, learning, discovery, and governance working together to turn regulatory data into trustworthy, actionable intelligence.

This is the philosophy behind Beyond Rules: Building AI-Driven Frameworks for Liquidity Reporting.
